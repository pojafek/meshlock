Meshlock / A note for Farrar & Tanner

One thing I noticed

Your customers type their message once. Then it gets typed again.

I'm Kamil. I manage production at a UK manufacturer, and on the side I build small tools for firms I'd like to work with. I looked at Farrar & Tanner from the outside: your sites, your film and your Companies House filings. One thing stood out, and I think it is worth a few minutes of your time.

Why this note is so detailed: it isn't a mass mailing. I only write to a firm after doing the homework, when I think I can genuinely help. I'd rather show you what I see than send you a brochure.

What I saw

Personal, fast and busy

Nearly everything across your six brands can be personalised, and you promise same-day engraving and dispatch.

Source: your careers page

Your Personalise Your Gift film shows the message typed into the engraving software at the machine, then the item placed and the job started.

Source: your own film

At checkout the customer picks the position, types the text, chooses one of six fonts and confirms against a preview. A pocket watch can carry three lines on the front and three on the back.

Source: your product pages

About 4,000 lines, 39 people, engraving, embossing and embroidery, and the busiest weeks of the year starting now.

Source: accounts to May 2025 · press

The problem I'd expect

Typed twice means two chances to go wrong

The customer has already told you everything: the text, the font and the position, and has seen a preview. If those three are entered again by hand at the machine, each one is a chance to drift from what they approved. On a personal gift the customer cares about every character. A missing space, two hyphens instead of one, the wrong one of six fonts, a line on the front that belonged on the back. In December the same message is read and typed again hundreds of times a day, under pressure. Each slip is a remade branded item, a late parcel and an unhappy recipient.

Today, as I read it

Customer types the message → order → operator reads it, types the text again, picks the font and the position → place the item → engrave

Nothing retyped

Typed once

Customer types the message → order → text, font and position go into the product's template untouched → operator places the item, checks the preview, presses start

How I'd approach it

Around your setup, not inside it

1

Take the customer's text, font and position straight from the order, exactly as approved, from all six brands.

2

Sort the day's orders: same-day first, then by process and product, so each machine gets its own jobs in a sensible order.

3

Build each engraving job as a ready-to-run design file, one per side: the template for that product and position, every line where the customer put it, in the font they chose, turned into outlines so no font can be swapped on the way. Named so the operator knows what to put on the bed. What the customer saw in the preview is what gets engraved.

4

Drop the files, in order, into a folder your engraving software watches. Your operator still checks and releases every job. Nothing changes in your shop systems.

Where I'm coming from

This is a side interest, not an agency. I work full time in production, so I take on one or two firms at a time and build every tool myself. In my own plant, order data goes straight onto the paperwork and into the files, and a daily hour of sorting order paperwork now takes about a minute. I'm looking for a firm where I can make a real difference and stay for the long run. From the outside, yours looks like one. The first tool is paid for only when it works as agreed.

What I'm not saying

I haven't seen inside your building. Your setup may already pass the text through, or your team may be careful enough that it never bites. If so, ignore this note and good luck with December.

Does this ring true? Tell me how the text gets to the engraver today.
